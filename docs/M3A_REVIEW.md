# M3a review — statistical diagnostics and physical observables

M3a is implemented on `codex/m3a-statistical-diagnostics` from merged M2 (`ef96696`, PR #7). This feature branch has not been merged or deployed. The [specification](SPECIFICATION.md) and [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) define the scope. M3b saved experiments, sweeps and export/import remain deferred.

## Observable and measurement conventions

The M2 Hamiltonian remains `H=−Jx Σx sᵢsⱼ−Jy Σy sᵢsⱼ−h Σᵢsᵢ`, with `β=1/T`, `N=LxLy`, signed `m=M/N`, and physical energy density `e=H/N`. A retained observation follows segment-local warm-up and the selected number of complete nominal sweeps. The worker records `m`, `|m|`, `e`, `m²`, `m⁴`, `e²`, directional bond products and bounded axial pair products together. Parameter and sampler changes begin isolated measurement segments and preserve the numerical state/RNG; geometry changes and Reset start a new seeded session. No display interaction schedules a measurement or consumes RNG.

The finite-volume equilibrium identities are `χ=βN(〈m²〉−〈m〉²)`, `U₄=1−〈m⁴〉/(3〈m²〉²)` and `cV=β²N(〈e²〉−〈e〉²)`. χ uses signed magnetization, without replacing `〈m〉` by `〈|m|〉`. `cV` is heat capacity **per site** at fixed physical couplings and field. Sampled plug-in fluctuations use population variance (`M₂/n`), while the separately labeled sample variance uses `M₂/(n−1)`. Small-sample plug-in estimates can be biased; neither is an automatic uncertainty interval. Binder is N/A when `〈m²〉` is too small to support its denominator. Tiny negative variance attributable to cancellation is clamped only within a `10⁻¹²` tolerance; more severe invalid values fail.

`Gx(1)` and `Gy(1)` average spin products over **actual** positive-axis bond records, including both periodic length-two parallel bonds. Open axes divide by their actual bond counts. Spatial `Gα(r)` averages ordered origins with a positive displacement, wrapping periodically or omitting out-of-range open pairs. The worker evaluates `r=0,…,min(4,⌊Lα/2⌋)` for periodic axes and `r=0,…,min(4,Lα−1)` for open axes, only at retained measurements. Only **fully periodic** views also show `Gα(r)−〈m〉²` using the segment mean, not instantaneous `m²`. Open and mixed-boundary views show raw pairs. On a periodic-x/open-y cylinder with `Jx=0`, `Jy>0`, `h>0`, x columns are independent but row means vary; `Gx(r)−〈m〉²` can be positive even when the site-centered covariance is zero. This costs `O(Nr)` per retained observation, not per spin update. Structure factors, site-dependent connected centering and correlation-length fits are deferred.

The direct 16-state 2×2 physical-parameter enumerator now reports χ, Binder, cV and x/y bond products for open, periodic and mixed boundaries. It retains shifted log-sum-exp normalization across the complete M2 temperature/coupling/field range. Exact ensemble quantities and sampled plug-in estimates are displayed side by side; no large-lattice exact reference is implied.

## Empirical statistical record

Each retained M2 or small-graph Ising parameter segment owns three aligned 8192-value worker rings for `m`, `|m|` and `e` (M2 physical `H/N`) or `E=S` (Small Systems dimensionless total action). Their order and spacing follow the actual measurement sequence. Full-segment online moments, histograms and the 1000-point plot history remain separate. Analysis uses only the latest consecutive observations after ring eviction; the panel reports analyzed/full counts, first/last measurement numbers, eviction count and a **tagged cadence**. At most eight parameter segments remain. The run seed and identity are carried by the session snapshot; each segment carries its own physical parameters, sampler, warm-up and cadence.

For `n≥64`, use the recent mean `x̄` and `γ(k)=Σᵢ(xᵢ−x̄)(xᵢ₊ₖ−x̄)/(n−k)`, so `γ(0)` has denominator `n`. The normalized ACF is `ρ(k)=γ(k)/γ(0)`. At most `min(128,⌊n/10⌋)` lags are computed. The conventional reversible-chain initial-positive pairs are `Γ_j=γ(2j)+γ(2j+1)`, beginning with `γ(0)+γ(1)`; an unmatched final lag is ignored. Their sign is identical when formed from ρ. The first nonpositive pair stops the usable sequence **before** that pair. Among positive complete-pair endpoints (odd W), use the first `W≥5 max(½,τ(W))` for `τ(W)=½+Σₖ₌₁ᵂρ(k)`. If no window exists by the lag cap, the record is unresolved. If a nonpositive pair intervenes first, at least five positive lags and `W≥3 max(½,τ(W))` permit only a provisional/window-sensitive estimate; an earlier cutoff suppresses τ, ESS and SE. Thus a noisy early negative pair is never reported as convergence. The convention follows [Geyer's initial-sequence definition](https://www.stat.umn.edu/geyer/mcmc/library/mcmc/html/initseq.html); the additional 5τ/3τ safeguards are this laboratory's finite-record policy, not a theorem. Insufficient data, nearly constant values, apparent first/second-half drift, unresolved tails and sensitivity to halving W receive explicit statuses. Drift also suppresses τ, ESS and SE. The drift comparison is a heuristic warning, not a stationarity test.

When available, `ESS=min(n,n/(2τ))`, with noisy `τ<½` floored at `½`, and `SE≈√(2τs²/n)` uses `s²` with denominator `n−1`. These values refer to the **recent diagnostic-window mean**, never automatically to the longer full-segment mean. For M2, τ is shown in retained observations and also multiplied by the configured stride in nominal sweeps (`N` random-site updates per sweep). Graph local Ising observations are one elementary site update apart: `τ_updates=τ_observations` and `τ_sweeps=τ_updates/N`, including `N=1`. Direct graph sampling consists of independent whole-configuration draws; τ is in draws and a nominal local-update sweep is N/A. The exact tiny-chain τ is computed at the same retained-observation interval and labeled accordingly. A rendered frame is neither a sweep nor physical time. Observed error against exact theory, estimator SE, warm-up bias, autocorrelation and finite-size effects are distinct.

A blocking cross-check uses nonoverlapping blocks of sizes 1, 2, 4, …, discards each incomplete remainder and displays `√(sample variance of block means / block count)` only with at least 16 blocks. No automatic plateau is claimed. ACF, blocking and checks are computed in the worker on measurement-count milestones: observation 1, multiples of 16 through 256, then multiples of 256. React receives compact summaries and at most 129 ACF points per observable, not raw rings; a displayed full-segment count can be ahead of the latest analysis refresh. These calculations do not draw random numbers.

## Exact tiny-chain benchmark

For every valid 1–4-spin graph Ising model, the exact one-update transition matrix uses the existing graph energy/local-field calculations. It reproduces direct independent Gibbs draws, random-site ½-lazy Metropolis (including proposal stays/rejections), or random-site heat-bath resampling (including unchanged outcomes). Distinct bonds and self-links retain their established action contribution. For M2 2×2, the matrix uses the physical Ising energy divided by T and all boundary choices. Row stochasticity, exact Gibbs stationarity and detailed balance are checked in tests and before numerical spectral claims.

For resolvable Gibbs weights, the reversible matrix `diag(√π) P diag(1/√π)` is diagonalized by a small symmetric Jacobi method. The elementary-update spectral gap is `1−λ₂`. Centered observable projections give exact stationary `ρ(k)` and asymptotic variance `Σₐ cₐ²(1+λₐ^q)/(1−λₐ^q)`, from which exact `τ_int=asymptotic variance/(2 equilibrium variance)` follows. Here `q=1` for graph observations and `q=N×strideSweeps` for M2 observations. This mode formula is equivalent to the finite-state Poisson-equation result; an independent linear solve validates representative cases. If a Gibbs probability is below `10⁻¹²`, a mode is effectively closed, the spectrum is unstable, or the observable is constant, the interface says so instead of claiming a trustworthy exact time. Exact stationary theory does not prove a single trajectory has mixed.

## Validation and performance

Numerical tests independently enumerate all 16 configurations for open, periodic and mixed 2×2 boundaries, including anisotropic, negative and nonzero-field cases; check bond multiplicity and sampled record alignment; and exercise invalid Binder/variance cases. Statistical tests cover constants, independent data, stationary two-state and AR(1) sequences, strongly correlated/drifting records, factor-of-two τ/ESS conventions, graph-local and direct cadence, M2 stride conversion, early noisy cutoff and 8192-record eviction. A targeted 2×3 cylinder enumeration checks that global-mean centering can falsely imply correlation between independent columns; full-periodic and fully open cases verify the chosen display policy. Exact-kernel tests cover row sums, stationarity, detailed balance, lazy/self transitions, heat-bath unchanged probabilities, spectral gaps, independent Poisson-equation asymptotic variance, graph/M2 kernel equivalence and stationary trajectory ensembles. Worker tests cover seeded replay, Step/batch equivalence, warm-up exclusion, parameter isolation and bounds. Production browser tests exercise exact/sample observables, insufficient/constant statuses, graphical ACF, Metropolis/heat-bath diagnostics, diagnostic units, spatial boundary labels, quench isolation, display-only controls, and desktop, 360px Chromium phone and emulated iPhone WebKit workflows. The complete `npm run check` pipeline passed: formatting, typecheck, **234 unit tests in 28 files**, production build and **111 browser tests** across the three browser projects.

In a local production Chromium run with a two-second Run interval, the corrected branch completed about 192,512 updates on 16×16, 192,512 on 32×32 and 192,512 on 64×64, approximately 93 thousand updates/s including worker/UI scheduling. The initial M3a and M2 reviews measured roughly 92 thousand and 94–96 thousand updates/s, respectively, using the same style of short local observation. These differences are small relative to timing/scheduling noise; this is not a controlled hardware benchmark or physical-phone speed claim. The 8192-record cap, 128-lag cap, measurement-only `O(Nr)` spatial calculation and scheduled analysis bound the extra work. Near criticality, a chain can remain correlated beyond the available lag/window, so the UI may correctly decline an error estimate. Fixed warm-up never guarantees equilibrium.
